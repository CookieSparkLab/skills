# Visual language

## Core impression

Create a workshop-friendly diagram that feels like a bold Notion sketch translated into a modern Chinese presentation:

- rational structure
- friendly color blocking
- heavy black construction lines
- modest hand-drawn character
- high projection readability

The result should feel authored, not generated from a generic SaaS card grid.

## Palette

Use near-black for all typography and construction:

- ink: `#111111`
- muted ink: `#3D3D3D`

Choose 3–5 fills according to semantic roles:

- pink / human need: `#F8DFF0`
- blue / intelligence or system core: `#AFC8E9`
- mint / knowledge or experience: `#DCE8D7`
- red / tools, action, or urgency: `#E05A5A`
- yellow / outcome or highlight: `#F5C75B`
- cream / optional local panel: `#FFF8E7`

Do not fill the full canvas unless the user asks. Transparency is part of the style.

## Shape language

- Use broad rectangles, tabs, labels, underlines, arrows, and simple icon circles.
- Prefer 6–8 px black strokes with round joins.
- Use a subtle 2–4 px positional imperfection only for decorative paths.
- Keep content boxes aligned to a clear grid.
- Avoid wrapping every tiny label in a card.

## Typography

Recommended Chinese families:

- `Noto Sans SC`
- `Source Han Sans SC`
- `PingFang SC`

Use weight 800–900 for headings and 600–700 for supporting text. Use a monospace font only for short technical tokens.

## Layout families

- `flow`: a directed top-to-bottom or left-to-right process
- `hub`: one large coordinator connected to 3–5 resources
- `comparison`: two balanced sides with one shared conclusion
- `layers`: stacked levels showing dependency or abstraction
- `equation`: inputs + coordinator + resources → outcome
- `cards`: 3–5 peer concepts with one framing statement

Select structure from meaning. Do not force every topic into a flowchart.

## Anti-style

Reject:

- white background baked into an otherwise transparent asset
- pastel-on-pastel text
- overly thin strokes
- rounded SaaS dashboard cards
- gradients and glass effects
- ornamental arrows with unclear direction
- line labels sitting on the line
- decorative doodles competing with content
- dense paragraphs
